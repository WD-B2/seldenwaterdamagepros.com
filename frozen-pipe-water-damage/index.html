Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage in Selden, NY

A frozen pipe is a warning, and a thawed one can become a flood in minutes.

Call (934) 223-1875

If the faucets are dry in freezing weather, treat it as urgent, because a split pipe floods the moment ice melts. Our 24/7 line at (934) 223-1875 is open, and meanwhile open the nearby tap, keep the heat on, and know where your main valve is.

Frozen pipe water damage in Selden, NY is the water release that follows when ice in a supply line expands, splits the pipe, and then thaws. Most of the harm happens after the thaw, not during the freeze.

When a frozen pipe becomes an emergency

A pipe that is only frozen is a plumbing problem. A pipe that has split is a flood waiting for the temperature to rise. If you hear dripping, see a bulge in the pipe, or notice frost on a line that is also leaking, shut the main valve right now and call.

Thawing can start the water without warning. A split the length of your thumbnail can release gallons a minute once the ice plug gives way, so leave the faucet open and watch the area.

Why ice splits metal and plastic

Water expands about 9 percent as it freezes. That alone does not usually crack a pipe. The real damage comes from trapped pressure: ice blocks the line, the water between the ice plug and the closed faucet has nowhere to go, and the pipe wall gives way a few inches or feet from the ice itself.

That is why the split is often in a spot that looks fine when you inspect it. Once the plug melts, the pipe starts delivering water into whatever surrounds it. A different cause of failure, with a similar flood, is described on our burst pipe page.

Where pipes freeze first

Exterior walls

Pipes run close to cold siding with little insulation, especially behind kitchen and bathroom sinks.

Unheated spaces

Garages, attics and cold corners of a basement drop below freezing before the living space does.

Under-floor voids

Supply lines in an uninsulated crawl space sit in moving cold air, a different issue from crawl space water damage.

Outdoor spigots

A hose left attached traps water in the line and can split the pipe inside the wall.

Thawing a pipe without making things worse

  1. Open the faucet served by the frozen line so melting water can escape.
  2. Apply gentle heat with a hair dryer or a heating pad, starting near the faucet and moving back toward the ice.
  3. Never use a torch, propane heater or open flame on a pipe.
  4. If you cannot reach the frozen section or it is behind a wall, stop and call a licensed plumber.
  5. Once water flows, check along the pipe for wet spots and drips.

Empty houses and winter absences

Long Island gets nor'easters, and a cold snap that follows a power outage can drop a house below freezing. In Middle Island, 30.1 percent of households are single-person according to the 2020 Census, so a pipe may split while the only occupant is away. If you leave for a week, ask a neighbor to check the house, and see our page for Middle Island water damage if you live there.

What we do after the thaw

Crews pump out standing water, then use moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to find water inside the wall cavity. A pipe that split in a wall can wet the cavity from the split down to the floor plate, even when only a small stain shows. Drying follows with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, as laid out on our structural drying page.

Because supply water is clean at the start, wall materials often dry in place when we reach them quickly. Insulation and padding rarely do. Details on opening and repairing the wall are on our drywall repair page.

Insurance notes

A split from freezing is often treated as a sudden loss, but policies may have conditions, such as heat being maintained in the house. Ask your insurer how your policy applies, and keep photos and records of any repair. We document moisture readings and a written scope to support your claim.

Stay safe while you wait

  • Close the main valve if any pipe is leaking.
  • Keep the heat on and open cabinet doors under sinks.
  • Stay away from outlets and cords where water is spreading.
  • Switch off power to wet rooms only when the panel is dry and safe to reach, and phone 911 or the utility before anything else for gas or electrical danger.
  • Do not stand under a sagging ceiling.

Questions we hear in Selden

Do you handle frozen pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Selden?

Yes. We run 24/7 dispatch, with same-day response when crews are available, including after hours and weekends. We cannot name an arrival time, but we can talk you through shutting the water off while you wait.

Is it safe to thaw a pipe myself?

Gentle heat from a hair dryer is the common approach. Never use a flame, and stop if the pipe is inside a wall or you see any leaking.

How do I know if the pipe split?

Drips, a bulge, frost on a leaking line, or stains on a wall or ceiling after the thaw. If unsure, close the main valve and check the meter.

Do you repair the pipe?

No. A licensed plumber repairs it. We remove the water, dry the structure and document the loss.

How long does drying take after a frozen pipe?

Usually several days. Readings from moisture meters tell us when the wall and floor are dry.

Will my walls need to be torn out?

Sometimes. Clean-water wall cavities can often be dried in place, but wet insulation and soft drywall are removed.
