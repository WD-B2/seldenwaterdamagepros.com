Your first 24 to 72 hours after water gets in

Most of the decisions that shape a water loss are made in the first day. Wet drywall, insulation and subfloor begin to swell and stain within hours, and EPA guidance is to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth. The steps below keep people safe and keep the loss from growing.

  1. Stop the source. Close the fixture valve, or the main shut-off if you cannot find the leak. Your water bill shows whether SCWA supplies your home.
  2. Check the electrical panel. Cut power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. For sparks, a humming outlet or any gas smell, leave and call 911 or the utility first.
  3. Photograph everything before you move it. Take wide shots first, then close-ups of damaged materials and any visible waterline.
  4. Lift small valuables, rugs and electronics off wet floors. Leave heavy furniture alone if you are unsure how wet the floor underneath is.
  5. Call so a crew can plan extraction and set up drying equipment.
  6. Expect moisture readings on day one and again on each visit, so the drying record shows when materials reach their target.

By hour 24 to 72, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers should be running, and materials that cannot be dried, such as saturated insulation or soaked carpet pad, should be out of the house. Waiting several days for a plumber or an adjuster before drying begins lets a small loss grow into a rebuild.

Water damage repair Selden homes need, from extraction to rebuild

Water damage repair Selden crews carry out falls into four stages, and each stage has a different job. Skipping one is how you end up with paint bubbling over drywall that still reads wet on a meter.

Extraction

Pumps and extraction units pull standing water off floors and out of carpet and pad, which shortens the drying that follows.

Mitigation and drying

Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces while LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras show where the water went.

Cleanup

Wet insulation, soaked pad and damaged drywall come out, and surfaces are cleaned. Category 2 and 3 water means more material removed than clean water does.

Repair

Drywall, paint, flooring and trim go back once the structure meets drying targets. Rebuild work is scoped separately from the drying.

The pump-out stage has its own page on water extraction, and the equipment side, including moisture mapping and drying logs, is explained under structural drying and dehumidification. Drying time depends on the materials, the water category and the weather, so a crew sets a plan after the first readings instead of promising a fixed number of days.

How pricing and scope get decided

Price follows scope, and scope follows what the inspection and the meters find. A flooded finished room and a damp closet corner are very different jobs, so a number given over the phone before anyone has seen the water would be a guess. The scope and price are explained to you before any work starts.

  • Water category: clean, grey or black water changes what must be removed and how it is handled.
  • Affected area: how many rooms or floors the water reached, and how much of each is wet.
  • Materials: carpet, hardwood, drywall and plaster dry and fail in different ways.
  • Drying days and equipment: the number of air movers and dehumidifiers and how long they run.
  • Rebuild scope: whether the job ends at dry or continues through drywall, paint and flooring.

To get a scope started, call (934) 223-1875 and describe what you see. The crew will confirm the details on site.

Insurance and documentation

Policies differ, so check yours or ask your insurer. Sudden and accidental damage, like a pipe that bursts, is often covered. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood usually needs a separate flood policy.

We document the loss with dated photos, moisture readings and a written scope, which supports your own claim. We do not decide coverage and cannot promise what any insurer will pay.

Choosing a service by where the water came from

Start with the source, because it decides what else is wet. A burst supply line or a leaking water heater usually floods one zone quickly, while frozen pipes and slab leaks can soak a structure for hours before anyone notices. A plumber repairs the pipe, and we handle the water that came out of it.

Dishwashers, washing machines, refrigerator lines and air conditioner drain pans leak slowly behind panels and under floors. The appliance leak page covers those, and the kitchen and AC pages cover the cabinets and ceilings they soak. Slide a dry paper towel under the appliance and check it with a flashlight before assuming the floor is dry.

Water from outside or above brings other problems. A stained or sagging ceiling belongs with ceiling water damage repair, wind-driven rain through windows or siding with storm water damage, and water in a below-grade room with basement water damage. Roof repair itself is a roofer's trade.

Standing water and wet framing get the extraction and drying stages above no matter how the water arrived. Sewage backup is a separate case because the water is Category 3, so stay out of it and call. Once the water is gone, the surface decides the repair, and hardwood floors can cup in days if moisture stays trapped under them. Carpet, drywall and plaster each have their own page.

Shops, offices and multi-unit buildings add downtime and tenants to the problem, which is why commercial water damage restoration is planned around business hours and a clear written record.

Mold comes last on purpose, because it follows moisture. Fix the water problem and dry the structure first. If mold is already visible or a musty smell lingers after drying, mold remediation covers containment, removal and the New York rule that licensed assessors and remediators cannot be the same individual on one property. The full list sits on the water damage services page.

When to call right away instead of waiting for morning

  • Water is still running or still rising
  • Water has reached more than one room or a second floor
  • Water is near the electrical panel, outlets or a ceiling light fixture
  • A ceiling is bulging, sagging or dripping heavily
  • Sewage or dark, foul water is involved

Any one of those is an emergency, and dispatch runs around the clock including nights, weekends and holidays. Same-day response is offered when crews are available. No arrival time can be promised, but the dispatcher will tell you what to do while you wait.

Stay out if the structure is at risk

Do not enter a room with a sagging ceiling or standing water near live wiring. For a gas smell or electrical danger, get out and call 911 or the utility first.

Questions we hear in Selden

What drying equipment is used, and how long does drying take?

A typical setup uses air movers, LGR dehumidifiers, moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, with HEPA air scrubbers added when dust or contaminants are a concern. The meters track each wall, floor and ceiling until readings drop to the target for that material.

Many clean-water jobs need several days of equipment, and hardwood, plaster and Category 3 losses can take longer. A crew gives you a realistic plan after the first readings, not before.

Is water damage restoration the same as basement waterproofing?

No. Restoration removes water that has already entered and dries and repairs what it damaged. Waterproofing is exterior or foundation work that keeps water from entering in the first place, and we do not offer it.

If your basement keeps taking in water, call a waterproofing contractor for the cause and us for the cleanup. Ask the Town of Brookhaven Division of Building about permit rules before any foundation work.

What if the loss is large and my insurer needs documentation?

Large losses go better with a paper trail. We record dated photos, moisture readings, the equipment placed and a written scope of work, and you can hand those to your insurer along with your own receipts.

Coverage is decided by your policy and your insurer, not by us. Read your policy, ask about deductibles and flood coverage, and keep damaged items until your insurer says they can be discarded.

Do you handle emergencies at night and on weekends?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, and crews are dispatched for active leaks, flooding and sewage backups at any hour. Same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and no specific arrival time is promised.

While you wait, stop the water if you safely can, protect the panel and move valuables up.

Should I call a plumber or a restoration company first?

Do both, in whichever order gets the water stopped sooner. A plumber fixes the pipe, fixture or heater. We remove the water and dry the building, and we are not a plumbing, roofing or electrical company.

If you cannot reach a plumber, shut off the main valve and call us so drying can start while you wait.

Can I start cleaning up myself before the crew arrives?

You can stop the source, mop up small amounts of clean water and lift items off wet floors. Do not run a household fan over sewage, walk through water near live wiring or pull out wet drywall without knowing what is behind it.

Take photos before you throw anything away. Wet materials are part of your claim record.

What does water damage restoration cost in Selden?

It depends on the water category, the area affected, the materials involved, the days of drying equipment and how much rebuild is needed. A short pipe leak caught early costs a fraction of a loss that sat for a week.

The scope and price are explained before work starts, and you decide whether to proceed.

Is mold removal part of water damage restoration?

Drying quickly is the best way to keep mold from starting, but removing existing mold is a separate job with its own rules. New York requires licensed mold assessors and remediators, and the same individual may not do both on one property.

Ask to see the licence of anyone who quotes mold work, and ask a physician about any health questions.

Do you only serve Selden?

Selden is home base, with crews also serving Mount Sinai, Middle Island and nearby places such as Coram, Centereach and Farmingville. Availability depends on crew schedules at the time you call.

Give the address when you phone and the dispatcher will confirm whether the crew can reach you the same day.
