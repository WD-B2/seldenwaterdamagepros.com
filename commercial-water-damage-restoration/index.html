Commercial water damage restoration is the removal of water and the controlled drying of a shop, office, restaurant or multi-unit building so it can reopen with as little lost time as possible. Here is how that works in Selden, NY.

When water in your building is an emergency

Not every wet spot is a crisis. A damp ceiling tile under a condensation drip can wait for a morning phone call. These situations cannot:

  • Water running from a ceiling or wall while the supply is still on
  • Standing water near electrical panels, floor outlets or server racks
  • A failed sprinkler line, boiler or rooftop unit
  • Sewage or grey water backing up from a floor drain or restroom
  • Ceiling tiles bowing under the weight of trapped water, which can mean a sag or collapse

In any of those cases, call right away, day or night. Same-day response is the aim whenever crews are free, and you get a plain read on timing when you call instead of a promised window. If sewage is involved, our sewage backup cleanup page explains the extra safety steps.

Why downtime shapes the whole job

A restaurant loses a service day, an office loses desks, and a retail floor loses sales. So the plan starts with one question: which rooms must reopen first? We map moisture with meters and a thermal imaging camera, then dry in zones so dry rooms stay usable while wet ones get air movers and LGR dehumidifiers. In a kitchen, ask your local health authority what you must show before reopening.

Plazas, shared walls and the unit next door

Middle Country Road (NY 25) carries most of Selden's commercial development, and plazas such as College Plaza (1975), Selden Plaza (1978) and Independence Plaza (1992) sit along it. Plaza-style buildings often put several units under one roof line. That means water from one unit can run along a shared wall or ceiling cavity into the next, so we check neighboring units before calling a job dry. The stretch around Boyle Road also has its own page for Downtown Selden.

Tell your landlord or property manager as soon as you see water, so adjoining tenants are warned. If the source is above you, a licensed roofer or plumber handles it, and our ceiling water damage repair work covers what fell or stained below.

How the dry-out runs

  1. Stop the source and photograph the damage before anything moves.
  2. Pull standing water with pumps and extraction units, as described on our water extraction page.
  3. Remove what cannot dry in place, such as wet ceiling tile, insulation and baseboard.
  4. Set air movers and dehumidifiers, then record moisture readings every day.
  5. Release the space only when readings match dry reference points, then scope the rebuild.

The daily readings are the proof the job is done, and they add to the paper trail for your claim. It is the process we describe under structural drying, scaled up.

What drives the cost of a commercial dry-out

  • Water category, from clean supply water to sewage
  • Square footage and number of units or floors affected
  • Materials involved, such as slab, tile, drywall or finished millwork
  • Drying days and how much equipment the space needs
  • Rebuild scope, including ceilings, paint and flooring

Phased work around your business hours also changes the schedule. Call us and we will walk the space, explain the scope and the price before work starts, and put the plan in writing.

Insurance and the building owner

Commercial property policies often cover sudden and accidental water damage, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not covered. Flood usually needs separate flood coverage. Some policies add business income coverage, but check your policy or ask your insurer. If you rent, ask your landlord which policy covers the building and which covers your contents. We document damage with photos, moisture readings and a written scope to support your claim.

What we do and what we hand off

We are a water damage restoration company, not a plumbing, roofing or electrical contractor. Call a licensed plumber for the failed line, a roofer for the leak above, and an electrician before power returns to wet circuits. We can coordinate the dry-out around their visit, including jobs in nearby hamlets such as Middle Island along the NY 25 corridor.

Stay safe while you wait

Before the crew arrives

If you smell gas or see sparking, leave and call 911 or the utility first. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach.

Keep people out of rooms with sagging ceilings, standing water near outlets, or sewage. Do not walk through contaminated water, and do not run a wet-dry vacuum on standing water near electrical equipment.

Questions we hear in Selden

Do you handle commercial water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Selden?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, and we aim for same-day response when a crew is available. We do not promise an arrival time, but you will get a straight answer about timing when you call.

While you wait, stop the water if you can do so safely, lift stock off the floor and keep people out of affected rooms.

Can my business stay open during drying?

Often partly. We dry in zones, so rooms with no moisture can stay in use. Equipment noise and keeping customers out of wet areas are the usual limits.

What should I document for a commercial claim?

Photograph the source, the water and every affected room before anything moves. List damaged stock and equipment, and keep receipts for emergency steps. Our moisture readings and written scope add to that record, and your insurer decides what is covered.

The water came from the unit next door. What now?

Tell your landlord or property manager and the neighboring tenant at once, and photograph everything on your side. Responsibility often depends on your lease and the policies involved, so ask the building owner and your insurer. We can dry your space while that gets sorted.

How long does a commercial dry-out take?

Wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours of the loss where possible. Full drying of a slab or wall cavities can take several days, and daily readings tell us when it is done. Larger or contaminated losses take longer.
