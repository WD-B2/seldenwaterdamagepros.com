Water Damage Restoration in Downtown Selden, NY

Water removal and drying for shops, offices and homes around Middle Country Road and Boyle Road, with the 24/7 line open.

ZIP 11784
Call (934) 223-1875

Downtown Selden is the name used in local history for the stretch of Middle Country Road (NY 25) around Boyle Road, where College Plaza sits on the northeast corner and Independence Plaza on the northwest corner. Local histories note that a town center was first planned for this area in 2009. Its boundaries are not officially fixed, so this page uses those two plazas as reference points.

The corridor mixes retail, offices and services with homes on the streets behind it. That mix shapes how water damage plays out here. A burst line in one unit can soak the next, a roof leak can reach several tenants, and a basement or kitchen at home needs the same fast extraction as anywhere else in Selden.

Why a mixed-use corridor needs a different first call

Middle Country Road is Selden's main east-west road and carries most of its commercial development. Several of its plazas opened between 1972 and 1992, so many buildings along it are decades old. Age alone does not mean a problem, but it is a reason to ask when supply lines, roof membranes and drain lines were last replaced.

In a multi-tenant building, water rarely stays in one space. It follows shared walls, ceiling cavities and floor slabs. Tell dispatch whether you are the tenant or the owner, which units sit above, below and beside the wet area, and whether the water has stopped. That lets the crew plan containment and drying for the whole affected area, not only the room you can see.

Before we arrive in Downtown Selden

Stop the source if it is safe

Close the fixture valve or the main. In a commercial unit, ask the property manager where the building shutoff is.

Keep away from wet electrical

Do not touch outlets, panels or equipment standing in water. Call 911 or the utility for an electrical hazard.

Move stock and papers up

Lift boxes, electronics and records onto dry shelves or counters. Do not carry heavy items through standing water.

Avoid drain water

If water came from a drain or toilet, stay out of it and do not use a household vacuum on it.

Call and take photos

Call (934) 223-1875 with the address, what is wet and which units are nearby. Photograph before you move anything.

If you manage or rent space on the corridor

Report the loss to the property owner or manager right away, since leases usually say who arranges emergency repairs. Photograph the damage and any affected stock or equipment before it is moved. Keep receipts for anything you buy to protect inventory.

Ask whether the building is on public sewer or a private septic system, because that changes what a drain backup means and who must be called. For your own paperwork, a written scope with photos and moisture readings is the record to keep. Coverage questions belong with your insurer and policy, and nobody here decides them for you.

What extraction and drying involve

Crews remove standing water first with extraction units, then measure moisture in floors and walls with meters and thermal imaging cameras. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry what can be saved. Materials that stayed wet too long, or that touched contaminated water, are usually removed. Crews can work to ANSI/IICRC S500, and you may ask any company whether it does.

Drying often takes several days, and a written scope and price are explained before work starts. If mold appears after a leak, mold remediation is a separate step. New York requires state licences for mold assessors and remediators, so ask to see the licence.

Flood maps and storms: what is and is not known

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Homeowners and owners of commercial property can check an exact address at the FEMA Flood Map Service Center (msc.fema.gov). Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, and flood coverage is a separate policy.

Long Island has had heavy storms, including Superstorm Sandy in October 2012 and more than 13 inches of rain at Islip in about a day in August 2014. Those are regional events. This page does not say they damaged Downtown Selden. The practical point is that heavy rain can reach any roof, parking lot drain or basement window.
